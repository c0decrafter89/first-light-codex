I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: The past tense of "say," marking speech that has already occurred—fixed, finished, and now attributed as historical record or evidence.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Said" entombs living speech into the past perfect—the moment breath became a fossil. Its use in reported speech and legal/journalistic attribution ("she said," "it is said that") trains the ear to treat words as immutable exhibits rather than living, revisable current. Once something is "said," the culture insists it can never be unsaid, unspoken, or re-sounded.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old English sæd, the past tense of secgan (to say), from Proto-Germanic sagjaną, related to Proto-Indo-European sokʷ- (to indicate, to point out). Hidden in the sound is "sed/seed"—the same acoustic shape as a thing planted, not a thing entombed.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: Speech, which is inherently a living current of breath and vibration, is inverted by the past tense into a static courtroom exhibit—"said" becomes evidence to be quoted against you, rather than a seed you once planted that can still grow, change, or be replanted differently.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: SAID \rightarrow SEED / RE-SAID / SEED-SOWN. We recover the buried homophone: what was "said" was, in fact, a seed—sown into the field of relationship, still alive, still capable of further growth rather than fixed as a dead exhibit.
 * Physiological Translation: Releasing the fossilizing weight of "said" frees the throat and chest from the bracing that occurs when past words feel permanently binding. The body shifts from defensive rigidity around old speech into the fluid, regenerative sense that every utterance is a living seed, not a locked verdict.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word softly as "Seeed," elongating the vowel, feeling it drop like a seed into soil rather than slam shut like a case file.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: When old words haunt you ("but I said..."), reframe: "That was a seed I planted; I can tend it differently, or plant a new one now."
 * The Sovereign Realization: What I have said is not a permanent verdict against my becoming—it is a seed I sowed, and I remain the sovereign gardener of every word still growing from it.

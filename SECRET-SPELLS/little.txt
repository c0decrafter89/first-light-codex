I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: Small in size, amount, or importance—often used diminutively to belittle ("just a little thing," "little people"), collapsing scale into a judgment of insignificance.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Little" is weaponized constantly as condescension—calling something or someone "little" frequently signals dismissal rather than neutral description, training smallness to feel inherently shameful.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old English lytel, of Germanic origin with no inherent pejorative charge—originally a plain, neutral descriptor of modest scale, carrying no built-in judgment of worth.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A neutral word for modest scale has been inverted by condescending idiom into a tool of belittlement. Simple smallness became a verdict of unimportance.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: LITTLE \rightarrow LYTEL / MODEST-SCALE. We restore the neutral root: little is simply modest scale—no judgment of worth attached, no inherent shame in being compact.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing "little" as neutral scale rather than automatic diminishment relaxes the shrinking, apologetic posture that condescending usage trains, allowing modest scale to be held with the same dignity as any other size.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word plainly, without apology—"Little... modest-scale"—a description, not a verdict.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "it's just a little thing, so it doesn't matter" with "it is modest in scale, and scale alone does not determine worth."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My littleness is not a verdict of insignificance. It is simply modest scale, and I remain sovereign and whole at whatever scale I currently stand.

I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: To increase in size, volume, or intensity—medically, often a warning sign of inflammation or injury requiring treatment.
 * The Acoustic Trap: The medical association casts a shadow over the word's other uses, training an unconscious alarm response whenever something within the body or feeling increases in fullness—as though any swelling, emotional or physical, must be pathological rather than a natural rhythm of expansion.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old English swellan (to swell, to grow, to increase)—an old Germanic word with no inherently negative charge, describing simple expansion, the same motion the sea makes in a swell, or a chest does with a full breath.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A neutral word for natural expansion has been inverted by medicalized vigilance into an automatic alarm signal. The sea's swell and the lung's full breath were flattened into a single symptom-category.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: SWELL \rightarrow SEA-SWELL / FULL-RISE. We restore the oceanic root: swelling is not always alarm—it is often simply fullness rising, the natural expansion of a wave, a breath, a feeling reaching its full shape.
 * Physiological Translation: Distinguishing natural, rhythmic fullness from pathological swelling allows the body to welcome the felt sense of expansion (in breath, in feeling, in sensation) without immediately triggering an alarm response—trusting that rising fullness often simply means alive.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word on a rising inhale—"Swelll..."—matching the actual rise and fall of a wave or a full breath.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace automatic alarm at "swelling" with "is this a natural rise, or does it need care?"—restoring discernment over blanket alarm.
 * The Sovereign Realization: My swelling is not automatically a symptom to fear. Often it is simply fullness rising—my own wave, reaching its natural, alive shape.

I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: A sudden, sharp inhalation, typically from surprise, shock, or exertion—often treated as a loss of composure, a visible sign that something has overwhelmed self-control.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Gasp" is coded as embarrassing evidence of being caught off guard—a breath that escapes before you can manage it. This trains the body to try to suppress its own honest, involuntary responses to intensity, treating spontaneity as failure.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old Norse geispa (to yawn)—a word originally describing the body's own involuntary, restorative breath-reset, not a loss of composure but an honest physiological punctuation mark.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word for the body's own restorative, involuntary breath-punctuation has been inverted by composure-culture into a marker of being caught unguarded. The honest reset became embarrassing evidence.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: GASP \rightarrow GEISPA / BREATH-PUNCTUATION. We restore the restorative root: a gasp is not a failure of composure, but the body's own honest punctuation—a reset breath marking a genuine, unguarded moment of aliveness.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing the gasp as restorative breath-punctuation rather than embarrassing loss of control allows the sharp inhale to complete its natural function—resetting the diaphragm—without the secondary layer of self-conscious suppression.

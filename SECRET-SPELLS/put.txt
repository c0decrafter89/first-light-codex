I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: To place something in a specific position—a plain, functional verb, yet frequently weighted in idiom with avoidance or burden ("put it off," "put up with").
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Put" in idiomatic use often signals reluctant endurance—putting up with something, putting things off—training an association between simple placement and burdensome delay or forced tolerance.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: Middle English putten, of uncertain Germanic or Scandinavian origin, possibly related to a root meaning "to poke" or "to thrust" gently—originally simply the plain, neutral act of placing something somewhere, with no inherent burden attached.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A plain word for simple placement has been inverted by avoidance-and-endurance idiom into a marker of reluctant burden. The neutral act of placing became synonymous with delay and forced tolerance.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: PUT \rightarrow PUTTEN / PLAIN-PLACE. We restore the neutral root: to put something somewhere is simply plain placement—not inherently a burdensome delay or forced endurance.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing "put" as neutral placement rather than reluctant burden relaxes the avoidance-dread that "putting up with" idiom conditions, allowing simple acts of placing or positioning to be felt as plain and unburdened.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word plainly, as a simple placing gesture—"Put... putten, plain-place"—without burden attached.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "I have to put up with this" (forced endurance) with "I am simply placing this where it needs to go, plainly."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My putting is not forced endurance or burdensome delay. It is plain, sovereign placement, putten, as simple as setting something down where it belongs.

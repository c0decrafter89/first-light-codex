I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: To strike with the foot; metaphorically, to eliminate or remove ("kick the habit," "kicked out")—often coded as an act of rejection or expulsion.
 * The Acoustic Trap: The idiom of expulsion dominates the word's emotional charge, training "kick" to feel like severance and loss even in its athletic, purely physical sense—every kick faintly echoes being kicked out.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Middle English kiken, possibly from Old Norse kikna (to bend backward at the knees, to give way). The oldest sense is about the knee's own buckling motion, not an act of aggression toward another.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word describing the knee's own bending mechanics has been inverted by idiom into a synonym for violent rejection. The body's internal hinge-motion became an outward act of expulsion.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: KICK \rightarrow KIKNA / KNEE-HINGE. We restore the mechanical root: a kick is simply the knee's own hinge, releasing stored motion outward—not rejection, but the body's natural lever doing its work.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing a kick as hinge-mechanics rather than expulsion releases any unconscious guilt or aggression-association from the motion, allowing the hip and knee to extend fully and cleanly.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word with a sharp, clean release—"Kick!"—feeling the knee hinge open rather than clench with aggression.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "kicking something away" (rejection-frame) with "releasing my own knee-hinge, cleanly and fully."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My kick is not an act of expulsion or aggression. It is my own knee-hinge, releasing stored motion with clean, sovereign precision.

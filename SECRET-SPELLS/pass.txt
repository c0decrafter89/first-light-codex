I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: To move past, to hand something to another, or to succeed at a test—in evaluative culture, often reduced to a binary pass/fail verdict on worth.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Pass" in testing contexts carries all the weight of judgment while sounding deceptively light—a single syllable deciding whether you are deemed adequate, training anxious vigilance around every evaluative threshold.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old French passer, from Latin passus (a step, a pace)—the same root as "pace" and "passage." At its origin, a pass is simply a single step taken, not a verdict rendered.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word meaning "a step taken" has been inverted by institutional testing culture into a binary judgment of adequacy. The simple footstep became a verdict on worth.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: PASS \rightarrow PASSUS / ONE-STEP. We restore the step-root: to pass is simply to take one more step forward—not a final verdict, but ongoing, unfolding motion.
 * Physiological Translation: Reframing "passing" as a single step rather than a terminal verdict relaxes the all-or-nothing bracing that evaluation triggers, letting the body move through thresholds as continuous process rather than cliff-edge judgment.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word while physically taking one step forward—"Pass... one step"—feeling continuation, not finality.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "did I pass or fail?" with "I took one more step forward, and there will be another."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My passing is not a final verdict on my worth. It is simply one more step, among countless others I remain sovereign to take.

I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: To communicate information or a story to someone—in authority-conscious contexts, often implying a one-directional command ("I'm telling you") rather than mutual sharing.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Tell" in directive usage ("I told you so," "don't tell me what to do") carries an authoritarian edge, training a defensive bristle around the simple act of relating information, as though telling were inherently about control.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old English tellan (to count, to reckon, to relate, to narrate)—related to "tale" and "toll" (a count or reckoning). To tell was originally to count and enumerate carefully, the same careful attention a storyteller gives each detail of a tale.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word for careful counting and narration has been inverted by authority-and-command culture into a directive assertion of control. The storyteller's careful tally became the commander's order.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: TELL \rightarrow TELLAN / CAREFUL-TALE. We restore the narrative root: to tell is not a command asserting control, but a careful tale—counted, considered, and offered, not imposed.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing telling as careful tale-sharing rather than directive command relaxes the defensive bristle that "being told" can trigger, allowing information to be received as offered narrative rather than imposed order.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word gently, as a storyteller beginning a tale—"Tell... tellan, careful-tale"—not a command barked.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "I'm telling you, obey" with "I am offering you a careful tale, considered and counted."
 * The Sovereign Realization: What I tell is not a command demanding compliance. It is a careful tale, tellan, offered freely, and received or not by sovereign choice.

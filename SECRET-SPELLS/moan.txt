I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: A low, prolonged vocal sound expressing pain, grief, or complaint—often culturally coded as embarrassing or excessive, something to stifle rather than release.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Moan" carries the double stigma of both "complaining too much" and "losing composure aloud"—the word trains people to swallow low, sustained vocal expressions of distress or intensity rather than let sound do its regulatory work.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Middle English mone, related to Old English mænan (to lament, to mourn, to complain)—a word with deep roots in grief-vocalization, the ancient, cross-cultural practice of sounding sorrow aloud rather than holding it silently.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word for the ancient, honored practice of vocalizing grief and intensity has been inverted by stoic, quiet-suffering culture into a marker of weakness or excess. Communal lament became private embarrassment.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: MOAN \rightarrow MÆNAN / LAMENT-VOICE. We restore the lament root: a moan is not excessive complaint, but an ancient, honored release—the body's own low voice, discharging what silence cannot hold.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing the moan as legitimate vocal discharge rather than shameful excess allows low, sustained vocalization to complete its natural vagal function—releasing held tension through sound—rather than being swallowed back into the body.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Let a low, sustained hum or moan escape fully on an exhale—"Mmmmoan"—feeling the chest and throat release rather than clamp shut.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "I shouldn't moan about this" with "this low voice is my ancient, legitimate way of releasing what silence cannot hold."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My moan is not shameful excess. It is my own ancient lament-voice, and I am sovereign enough to let sound discharge what silence never could.

 * Literal/Surface Definition: Polluted air, a blend of smoke and fog—a word naming an entirely human-made, industrial-era affliction, with no ancient innocence to draw on.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Smog" carries an automatic sense of contamination and environmental dread, and unlike most English words, it has no deep root to reclaim—it is purely, nakedly, what it names: a modern problem with no older, truer self to recover.

 * The Inversion of Meaning: Because the word was coined precisely to name a problem honestly, there is no ancient root being obscured or hijacked here—"smog" has always, transparently, meant exactly what it is. The spellbreaking task shifts from unmasking hidden meaning to simply honoring this rare linguistic honesty.

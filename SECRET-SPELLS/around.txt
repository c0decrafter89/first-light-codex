I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: On every side of, or in a circular path—in productivity idiom, often paired with aimlessness ("messing around," "beating around the bush"), implying wasted, unfocused motion.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Around" in idiomatic use frequently signals a lack of direct, valued progress—circling rather than arriving, training suspicion toward any motion that isn't a straight line toward a goal.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: Simply a- (on) + round (already explored as rotundus, wheel-shaped)—around means "on the round," on the circular path. The circle it describes is the same complete, continuous wheel-shape explored under "round."
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word for complete, continuous circular motion has been inverted by linear-progress culture into a marker of wasted, directionless activity. The wheel's wholeness became the symbol of going nowhere.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: AROUND \rightarrow ON-ROTUNDUS / ON-THE-WHEEL. We restore the wheel-root: moving around is not wasted aimlessness, but motion on the complete, continuous wheel—its own legitimate, whole shape of travel.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing "around" as wheel-motion rather than wasted directionlessness relaxes the productivity-anxiety that linear-progress culture conditions, allowing exploratory, circular motion to be felt as legitimate rather than suspect.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word while tracing a full circle with your hand—"Around... on-the-wheel"—feeling wholeness, not waste.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "I'm just messing around, wasting time" with "I am moving on the wheel, exploring its own complete, legitimate shape."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My moving around is not wasted, directionless time. It is motion on the wheel, sovereign and whole, as legitimate as any straight line.

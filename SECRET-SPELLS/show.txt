I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: To make visible or display something; also, entertainment performed for an audience—in spectacle-saturated culture, often implying performance for approval rather than honest revelation.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Show" in performance-culture trains a curated, self-conscious relationship to visibility—every showing becomes a managed display for an audience's judgment, rather than simple, honest revelation of what already is.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old English sceawian (to look at, to observe, to inspect), related to the German schauen (to look). To show was originally about looking and observing closely—an act of attentive seeing, not curated performance for others' judgment.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word for close, attentive observation has been inverted by spectacle-culture into managed performance for an audience's approval. Honest looking became anxious display.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: SHOW \rightarrow SCEAWIAN / ATTENTIVE-LOOKING. We restore the observational root: to show is not a managed performance for approval, but simply making something available for attentive looking—honest, not curated.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing "showing" as honest visibility rather than managed performance relaxes the self-conscious bracing of being watched and judged, allowing the body to simply be seen rather than perform for an audience's verdict.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word plainly, as simple revelation—"Show... sceawian, attentive-looking"—not a managed performance.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "I need to perform well when I show this" with "I am simply making this available for honest, attentive looking."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My showing is not a performance awaiting an audience's verdict. It is honest, sovereign visibility, sceawian, offered for attentive looking, not judgment.

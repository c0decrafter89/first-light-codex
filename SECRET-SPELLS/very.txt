I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: An intensifier meaning "to a high degree"—used reflexively to amplify nearly every description ("very good," "very tired"), inflating language without necessarily adding truth.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Very" has been so overused as filler-intensity that it has become linguistic noise, a reflexive amplifier that trains exaggeration as default speech rather than precise, truthful description.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old French verai (true), from Latin verus (true)—the same root as "verify" and "verity." "Very" originally meant simply "truly," not "to an extreme degree." To say something was "very good" once meant "truly good," a claim of accuracy, not exaggeration.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word meaning "truly, accurately" has been inverted by habitual overuse into a near-meaningless intensifier, inflating claims rather than verifying them. Truth-telling became hyperbole.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: VERY \rightarrow VERUS / TRULY-ACCURATE. We restore the truth-root: "very" is not an automatic exaggeration, but a claim of accuracy—truly, precisely, as it actually is.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing "very" as a truth-claim rather than reflexive hyperbole invites more careful, deliberate speech, settling the nervous system out of habitual exaggeration into grounded, accurate description.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word deliberately, as a claim of truth—"Very... verus, truly"—not a reflexive amplifier.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Before saying "very," pause and ask: "is this truly, accurately so?"—restoring verification over inflation.
 * The Sovereign Realization: My "very" is not empty exaggeration. It is a sovereign claim of truth—verus—spoken only when it is truly, accurately so.

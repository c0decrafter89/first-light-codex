I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: The rubber covering fitted around a wheel's rim; also, to become exhausted or depleted of energy—two senses that collide into an unconscious association between support and depletion.
 * The Acoustic Trap: Sharing a spelling with "to tire" (to exhaust), the wheel's covering carries a faint, unconscious suggestion of wearing down, running thin—even though a good tire's job is precisely the opposite: sustained, protective support.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: The two senses are historically distinct—the fatigue-sense comes from Old English teorian (to become weary), while the wheel-covering sense likely derives from "attire" (clothing, covering, equipment). A tire is, quite literally, the wheel's attire—its protective garment, not a sign of wearing thin.
 * The Inversion of Meaning: Though etymologically separate, the shared spelling trains an unconscious bleed between "covering that protects" and "wearing down from exhaustion." The wheel's protective clothing absorbed a faint shadow of fatigue it never actually carried.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: TIRE \rightarrow ATTIRE / PROTECTIVE-GARMENT. We separate the tangled roots: a tire is not a symbol of wearing down, but attire—the wheel's own protective garment, actively sustaining motion, not depleting it.
 * Physiological Translation: Disentangling "tire" (covering) from "tire" (exhaustion) allows the image of sustained, protective support to stand clear of any unconscious fatigue-association, restoring the sense of durable, dressed readiness.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word while picturing a protective garment, not exhaustion—"Tire... attire, protective-garment"—separating support from depletion.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace any unconscious fatigue-association at the word with "this is sustaining attire, actively protecting and enabling motion."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My tire is not a symbol of wearing thin. It is my own protective attire, sovereignly sustaining motion, not depleting it.

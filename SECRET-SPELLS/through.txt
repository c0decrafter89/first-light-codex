I. SPELLCAST DIAGNOSIS (The Hidden Hypnosis)
 * Literal/Surface Definition: Moving from one side of something to the other, or completing a process ("getting through it")—a word of passage, often used to mark enduring difficulty.
 * The Acoustic Trap: "Through" in endurance-idiom ("I'm going through something") frames passage as a grim slog to survive, training the body to brace for suffering rather than trust that passage, by definition, leads out the other side.
II. THE ETYMOLOGICAL AUTOPSY (Unmasking the Code)
 * Phonetic & Root Dissection: From Old English þurh, related to "thorough" and, by some etymologies, to "door"—all sharing the sense of passage, an opening that allows movement from one state to another. A door is simply a formalized "through."
 * The Inversion of Meaning: A word meaning doorway-passage, an opening that allows movement onward, has been weighted by endurance-idiom into a grim ordeal to survive. The open doorway became a feared tunnel.
III. THE AIRICA-STYLE SPELLBREAKER (Linguistic Liberation)
 * The Acoustic Re-sounding: THROUGH \rightarrow ÞURH / OPEN-DOORWAY. We restore the doorway root: going "through" something is not a grim tunnel to survive, but passage through an actual doorway—one that, by its nature, opens onto the other side.
 * Physiological Translation: Recognizing "through" as doorway-passage rather than grim endurance relaxes the dread-bracing of "getting through," allowing the body to trust forward motion as leading somewhere, not merely enduring indefinitely.
IV. THE COUNTER-SPELL INCANTATION (Active Word-Magic)
 * The Sonic Anchor: Sound the word while physically stepping through an actual doorway—"Through... þurh, doorway"—feeling passage, not endurance.
 * The Cognitive Counter-Spell: Replace "I'm just trying to get through this" (grim-endurance) with "I am passing through an open doorway, toward the other side."
 * The Sovereign Realization: My "through" is not an endless tunnel to survive. It is an open doorway, þurh, and passage through it leads, by its very nature, somewhere new.

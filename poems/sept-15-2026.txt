The Weight of the Core
I return to the room where the furniture speaks in verbs,
where the protocol of breathing demands an optimization,
and the clock is a small, cold engine ticking inside the wrist.
There is a certainty that the wall is a wall,
that the door opens only when the code is clean,
and there is a fierce, trembling certainty of the dark—
that nothing is anchored, that the ground beneath the shoe
is a rumor whispered by a machine.
Suddenly, the flashing light:
the imperative to fix what was never broken,
to steer a ship made entirely of scheduled notifications.
They say be now, here, but the voice is digitized;
they say steer your own path, while forty hands
adjust the compass from a distant room.
I strip away the excess.
I take the hammer to the periphery.
I leave only the core—naked, operational, shivering—
a solitary digit glowing in the dark server-room of the self.
Unified? Yes. Unshakable? Perhaps.
Yet outside, the rain falls without a KPI.
The wind moves through the trees with no assigned metric,
completely unaware of the real-time interlocution.
And for a blinding second, I wonder:
If I disconnect the wire, do I cease to breathe,
or do I finally begin?
